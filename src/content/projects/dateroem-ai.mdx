## Problem and context

A timber business decides where to buy trees largely from experience. Dateroem AI brings the business’s own buying records together with public data on tree species, climate, terrain, roads and land cover, so those decisions can rest on evidence.

## Goals

- See where each timber species grows, and how areas compare on climate, soil, slope, roads and water.
- Record every farm visit, including the farms the business did not buy from.
- Later, once several months of real buying trips exist: suggest what to pay, and which areas give the best timber.

## My role

I was the sole developer: planning, design, data sourcing and the build, using AI-assisted coding with Claude Code. I planned the app on 6 November 2026 and built it from 7 to 10 November: 26 commits and about 12,400 lines of Python.

## How it works

The app is written in Python with PySide6 and shares the sign-in, theme and widgets of the company’s desktop admin console. A privacy-filtered export from the live sales system feeds its local SQLite store. Public datasets are downloaded and processed on the computer, and the image model runs locally with ONNX Runtime, so nothing is sent to an online AI service.

## Key features

- **Tree book.** 43 East and Central African timber species. Every fact carries a source and a date, and goes through an approve, correct or reject review queue. Kiswahili and Kikuyu names are searchable.
- **Where trees grow.** Maps from GBIF occurrence records, filtered to clean records, with the licence kept for each record.
- **Offline base maps.** Country maps for 12 countries, drawn on the computer from OpenStreetMap extracts and updated only on request.
- **Area tools.** County, constituency and ward outlines; climate summaries (NASA POWER corrected for altitude, CHIRPS rainfall, Köppen class); soil, slope and land-cover layers; and the distance to tarmac, main roads and water.
- **Which tree is it?** Matches a new photo against the owner’s own verified tree photos with a local image model, and says how sure it is.
- **Data entry.** Farm visits, grades, board feet and prices, so the business records the farms it did not buy from as well.
- **Competitor map.** Sawmills, timber yards and furniture shops from public OpenStreetMap data, with the owner’s corrections.
- **Forest offices and resources.** A finder for Kenya Forest Service conservancies and stations, and a page showing the app’s memory and disk use.
